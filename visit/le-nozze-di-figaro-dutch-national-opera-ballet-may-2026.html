Le nozze di Figaro

Opera·

Dutch National Opera & Ballet, Amsterdam

With Le nozze di Figaro, Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart composed a tragicomic classic that has been a favourite with audiences ever since it premiered in 1786. Mozart and his librettist Da Ponte switch effortlessly between light-hearted intrigue and existential questions. Kirill Serebrennikov’s stage direction brings the opera’s social criticism into sharp focus, giving it contemporary relevance.

Conflicted

Serebrennikov clearly had a lot to say. A lot. The first half was genuinely interesting... ringtones played on the harpsichord mad me laugh sooo much, a man on all fours (all grotesque, but the point!), Susanna playing everyone like a fiddle (it should really be called Le nozze di Susanna). Then came the art gallery. And the fake massacre. And the mirrors. I checked out somewhere around the third mirror. Kulchynska's Countess was stunning though — two arias that made the mirrors almost worth it.
