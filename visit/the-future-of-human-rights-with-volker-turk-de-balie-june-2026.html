JUN 2026

The Future of Human Rights – with Volker Türk

Talk·

De Balie, Amsterdam

UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Türk in conversation on the state and future of international human rights.

Intrigued

I arrived to this talk already thinking about AI in warfare and (cultural) genocide from other talks and books I've been reading lately. War, displacement, mass surveillance, culture erasure... different topics but at the root is the same problem: people's dignity (or lives!!!) treated as negotiable.

I wanted to come away from this talk hopeful -- this is why I attend and listen to these things, truthfully; I need some kind of north star, anchor to humanity -- but I did not. It was truly unsettling to hear someone whose job is to have answers sound like he was just improvving -- but that was also endearing in its own way, or he's the greatest actor. There was something in his honesty: the UN is in survival mode (my favourite Power comment on the UN: the UN is a building full of people); he admitted the pressure on Israel and the US hasn't been enough. He made sure to comment on small wins, which I thought was necessary: like one person freed from detention or a death penalty law that didn't pass. It's not proof that things are fine and okay, but it is proof that the thread hasn't totally snapped yet.

So to pull all of this together with some recent readings: Papa Francesco's Laudato Si'; Appelbaum's work on autocracies; Synder on tyranny and freedom. The pope's idea of a common home, and Türk's recurring theme of agency are the same thing: care for each other is not sentimental but structural. Appelbaum's accounts of authoritarian networks goes hand-in-hand with Türk's breakdown of systems (funding, the flow of arms, disinformation ) rather than individual villains. And Snyder's idea that freedom is something you build together, not something you're simply granted, reframes the "we don't know what to do" feeling as just the ongoing work, not a failure. Idk, it's like this constant exhausting work with so many things against us.

And so... the future of human rights according to this talk: It's a long, hard road. There's no shortcut. Only the daily choice to to pay attention and to speak up. (And to vote!)
