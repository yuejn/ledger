3:T927,<p>A grand story -- a pirate gets elected as the Doge over the aristocracy&#39;s objections. He had a secret affair, woman dies, baby disappears, woman&#39;s dad is mad; grudges abound. Twenty-years later, a wiser and older Simon is trying to hold a fractured city together -- when he finds his daughter, who is love with one of his rivals, and a class war between the patricians and plebeians he is stuck in the middle of.</p>
<p>A grand show! Italian palazzo grandeur, crown moulding, textured walls -- a step up from some of DNO&#39;s other staging. The visuals delivered all night: windows that could fake day turning into night; smoke and blinding lights; quick set changes that kept the momentum. I&#39;m a sucker for big choruses and this production delivered. </p>
<p>Singing was really impressive across the board. Amelia&#39;s voice was piercing, Fiesco&#39;s bass was moving, Adorno was a strong solo and harmonised really nicely with Amelia -- indicating that finally, they are Meant To Be, I guess. Paolo&#39;s villainy was a bit lol to me, mostly because the orchestra kept overshadowing him.</p>
<p>The hectic council chamber scene with the &quot;kidnapper&quot; getting cursed was a real highlight. I was in the nosebleeds so I lost detail in the facial expressions but the fear in the crowd madness vibe came through pretty loud and clear. </p>
<p>Story-wise, I enjoyed and appreciated the forgiveness-across-generations arc. And the final scene of Simon dying on the same slab from the opening scene landed really well. </p>
<p>Less fun: the constant hand-wringing over Maria &amp; Amelia&#39;s purity, raised in act one and again in act two at which point I was just like, &quot;ugh guys&quot; -- just made me think of how protecting a woman&#39;s chastity has always been a mechanism of controlling us.</p>
<p>The ending ran a liiiiiittle too long, but it was also really warm in the theatre and I was sitting right in between two people who kept coughing (an observation, I know no one WANTS to cough in the theatre); and Paolo just vanishes off to the scaffold with zero ceremony. I think the final scenes were staged wonderfully, with the city all dressed in black -- mourners, little angels of death calling Simon, the gravity of his fate. </p>
<p>Minor gripes aside, I would recommend it. A nice, grand opera. :-)</p>
