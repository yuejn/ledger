11:T885,<p>I arrived to this talk already thinking about AI in warfare and (cultural) genocide from other talks and books I&#39;ve been reading lately. War, displacement, mass surveillance, culture erasure... different topics but at the root is the same problem: people&#39;s dignity (or lives!!!) treated as negotiable. </p>
<p>I wanted to come away from this talk hopeful -- this is why I attend and listen to these things, truthfully; I need some kind of north star, anchor to humanity -- but I did not. It was truly unsettling to hear someone whose job is to have answers sound like he was just improvving -- but that was also endearing in its own way, or he&#39;s the greatest actor. There was something in his honesty: the UN <em>is</em> in survival mode (my favourite Power comment on the UN: the UN is a building full of people); he admitted the pressure on Israel and the US hasn&#39;t been enough. He made sure to comment on small wins, which I thought was necessary: like one person freed from detention or a death penalty law that didn&#39;t pass. It&#39;s not proof that things are fine and okay, but it is proof that the thread hasn&#39;t totally snapped yet. </p>
<p>So to pull all of this together with some recent readings: Papa Francesco&#39;s Laudato Si&#39;; Appelbaum&#39;s work on autocracies; Synder on tyranny and freedom. The pope&#39;s idea of a common home, and Türk&#39;s recurring theme of agency are the same thing: care for each other is not sentimental but structural. Appelbaum&#39;s accounts of authoritarian networks goes hand-in-hand with Türk&#39;s breakdown of systems (funding, the flow of arms, disinformation ) rather than individual villains. And Snyder&#39;s idea that freedom is something you <em>build together</em>, not something you&#39;re simply granted, reframes the &quot;we don&#39;t know what to do&quot; feeling as just the ongoing work, not a failure. Idk, it&#39;s like this constant exhausting work with so many things against us. </p>
<p>And so... the future of human rights according to this talk: It&#39;s a long, hard road. There&#39;s no shortcut. Only the daily choice to to pay attention and to speak up. (And to vote!)</p>
