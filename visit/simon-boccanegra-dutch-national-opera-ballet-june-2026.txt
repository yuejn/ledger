11:Ta94,<p>Simon, a pirate, gets elected as the Doge over the aristocracy&#39;s objections. However, he had a secret affair that produced a baby who disappeared ... and also that lady&#39;s dad is really upset with Simon. Lots of grudges, death, the potential for incest -- sorry, but this is an opera and you have to be prepared for all manner of things. However, the bulk of this opera is set twenty years later -- when he&#39;s trying to maintain cohesion and rule of law and he discovers his daughter who is, woe!, in love with one of his political rivals. </p>
<p>A grand show -- Italian palazzo grandeur, crown moulding (I&#39;m sorry but this has to be stated because it&#39;s so noticeable when there isn&#39;t ANY kind of detail in decor), textured beautiful walls -- a step up from some other designs I&#39;ve seen. The visuals delivered throughout the night: windows that passed the day into the night; smoke and blinding lights (OK this I can give up); quick snazzy and smooth set changes that kept up the momentum. I&#39;m a sucker for big choruses and this production delivered. </p>
<p>Singing was really impressive across the board. Amelia&#39;s voice was piercing (damn girl). Fiesco&#39;s bass was moving. Adorno was a strong solo and harmonised really nicely with Amelia -- indicating that finally, they are Meant To Be, I guess. Paolo&#39;s villainy was a bit lol to me, mostly because the orchestra kept overshadowing him.</p>
<p>The hectic council chamber scene with the &quot;kidnapper&quot; getting cursed was a real highlight. I was in the nosebleeds so I lost detail in the facial expressions but the fear in the crowd madness vibe came through pretty loud and clear. </p>
<p>Story-wise, I enjoyed and appreciated the forgiveness-across-generations arc. And the final scene of Simon dying on the same slab from the opening scene landed for me really well. Ik ben een easy audience. </p>
<p>Less fun was the constant hand-wringing over Maria &amp; Amelia&#39;s purity, raised in act one and again in act two at which point I was just like, &quot;ugh guys&quot; -- just made me think of how protecting a woman&#39;s chastity has always been a mechanism of controlling us.</p>
<p>The ending ran a liiiiiittle too long, but it was also really warm in the theatre and I was sitting right in between two people who kept coughing (an observation, I know no one WANTS to cough in the theatre); and Paolo just vanishes off to the scaffold with zero ceremony. I think the final scenes were staged wonderfully, with the city all dressed in black -- mourners, little angels of death calling Simon, the gravity of his fate. </p>
<p>Minor gripes aside, I would recommend it. A nice, grand opera. :-)</p>
